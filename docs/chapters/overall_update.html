Overall Update

We are excited to share where the Gulf of California Ocean Health Index currently stands. Expert Working Groups 1, 2, and 3 (including 3.1 - 3.3) are now complete, and the plots below show each goal’s current status by OHI region. If you would like, you can hover your mouse over each plot to compare regions and years. A score of 100 means that the current status is equal to the reference point.

This is a snapshot of work in progress. Scores, methods, and write-ups will continue to be updated as we finish pressures (including Safety & Security), finalize the goals, and complete the resilience layers. Specific data sources and reference points for each goal are described in detail in that goal’s executive summary.

Tourism & Recreation

The overall TR score is the average of Quantity, Quality, and Sustainability. A score of 100 is the ideal sustainable state. Open the component scores below to see what is pulling a region up or down. Specific data sources and reference points are described in detail in the executive summary.

  • English: TR Executive Summary
  • Spanish: TR Resumen ejecutivo
  • EWG 3.1 page: Tourism & Recreation
  • Goalkeeper page: Tourism and Recreation
Component scores

Specific data sources and reference points for each component are described in detail in the executive summary.

This is the Quantity component, which evaluates whether international and domestic tourism is reaching the targets set by Plan México for 2030.

This is the Quality component, which evaluates whether visits are safe and well supported by dining, cultural, and recreation services, relative to the best conditions observed in the Gulf.

This is the Sustainability component, which evaluates whether tourism is supported by certified hotels and beaches, quality jobs, and public investment at or above recent historical levels.

Clean Waters

The overall CW score is the average of Eutrophication, Pathogens, Chemicals, and Marine Debris. A score of 100 is the ideal clean-waters state. Open the component scores below to see what is pulling a region up or down. Specific data sources and reference points are described in detail in the executive summary.

  • English: CW Executive Summary
  • Spanish: CW Resumen Ejecutivo
  • EWG 3.3 page: Clean Waters
  • Goalkeeper page: Clean waters
Component scores

Specific data sources and reference points for each component are described in detail in the executive summary.

This is the Eutrophication component, which evaluates excess nitrogen reaching the coast from wastewater, agriculture, livestock, and shrimp aquaculture, with a score of 100 meaning zero nitrogen pollution.

This is the Pathogens component, which evaluates Enterococcus at Playas Limpias beaches, with a score of 100 meaning waters meet Mexico’s beach suitability standard.

This is the Chemicals component, which evaluates pesticide, pharmaceutical, hazardous-waste, untreated-wastewater, and vessel-hydrocarbon risk, with a score of 100 meaning no chemical pressure relative to the highest levels observed in the Gulf.

This is the Marine Debris component, which evaluates the risk of abandoned fishing gear (ghost gear), with a score of 100 meaning no ghost-gear pressure.
